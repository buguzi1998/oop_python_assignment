## Summary of findings

**Project 1 - Population.** Wakiso grows fastest (CAGR 6.5 %/yr) versus 4.6 % for Kampala and Gulu. On the 2022-24 hold-out, the CAGR model won for Kampala and Wakiso and the calibrated Fibonacci model narrowly won for the smaller districts; the linear trend was worst everywhere. About 4,560 extra primary classrooms are needed across the five districts by 2029 (Wakiso ~2,090, Kampala ~1,540). The raw Fibonacci-ratio model implies ~60 %/yr growth and is indefensible.

**Project 2 - Micro-grid.** The system is well posed (det = -5, cond = 5.83). A single vectorised solve is roughly 30-50x faster than a loop. 7 of 30 days are infeasible (negative battery draw); non-negative least squares limits unmet load to 4.5 kWh versus 11.2 kWh for clipping. The battery is the more volatile source (CV 0.78 vs 0.14) and about 58 % of the monthly cost (UGX ~109k). A linearly dependent third equation makes the hybrid system singular.

**Project 3 - Fish stock.** Long-run yield peaks at h = r/2 = 0.20, matching MSY = rK/4 = 1,000 t/week; h = 0.30 catches less and leaves the stock at a quarter of K. A raw variance threshold is unit-dependent and meaningless; CV is unit-free. Annual revenue CV is ~0.11 for every harvest rate, so biological risk (not price risk) separates the scenarios. An 8-week closed season costs revenue at h = 0.10-0.20 but helps at h = 0.30.

**Project 4 - Rainfall.** `math.cos` cannot measure similarity; the dot-product formula matches SciPy. Cosine similarity is high for every pair (scale-blind and rainfall is non-negative), while Pearson is near zero for Kampala-Gulu. Mbarara is bimodal (April, October), as expected for southern Uganda; Gulu is unimodal at a 25 % prominence threshold but bimodal at 20 % or lower (transition zone). Kampala is detected as unimodal, which contradicts the literature and is traced to weaknesses in the illustrative data. Advisory: Mbarara farmers plant beans in early March and early October.

**Project 5 - Taxi.** The Ntinda equilibrium fare is UGX 2,200 (76 passengers per trip-hour) against the current UGX 2,000, so there is excess demand of 10. In walk-forward backtesting, exponential smoothing beat the moving average and linear trend on every route, with the tuned alpha at the boundary value 1.0 (the latest value is the best predictor). Day-11 forecasts are 45, 64 and 50 passengers (UGX 90k, 320k, 150k), and with a 15 % buffer each route needs 1 vehicle. On a 60-day simulated series with a weekly cycle, seasonal-naive (MAE 2.7) is about three times better than the 3-day moving average (MAE 8.1).
